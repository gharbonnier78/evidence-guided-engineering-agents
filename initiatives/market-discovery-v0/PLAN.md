# Market Discovery v0 — evidence-guided plan

**Status:** draft experiment plan

## 0. Intent

Market Discovery v0 is a bounded experiment to discover whether organizations will pay for specific complex-engineering outcomes that can later support a broader human+AI capability ecosystem.

The objective is **not** to prove the 2039 vision. It is to obtain enough real evidence to decide what to continue, revise, stop or scale next.

## 1. Core question

> Can we identify a small set of organizations with observable complex-engineering problems, engage the right human decision makers, and convert at least one problem into a paid, bounded intervention with measurable client value and positive or plausibly positive contribution margin?

## 2. Claims to test

### C-MD-001 — Problem visibility
Public and legally usable information contains enough signal to identify organizations with concrete, non-trivial engineering problems without first buying an expensive commercial prospecting database.

### C-MD-002 — Problem/Capability fit
At least one currently available or realistically developable capability can address a meaningful part of those problems better than a generic consulting conversation.

### C-MD-003 — Willingness to engage
Qualified organizations will agree to discovery conversations when outreach is tied to a specific observable problem rather than a broad “AI/ML/engineering” pitch.

### C-MD-004 — Willingness to pay
At least one organization will pay for a bounded diagnostic, engineering sprint or similar first intervention.

### C-MD-005 — Economic viability
At least one paid intervention can produce useful evidence of a sustainable contribution margin after external expertise, paid data, compute/AI and other variable costs.

### C-MD-006 — Reusability
At least part of the workflow can be reused across multiple organizations without degrading problem specificity or human accountability.

### C-MD-007 — Capability ecosystem value
Some discovered opportunities will expose repeatable needs for scarce human expertise, connectors, data or tools that could justify future capability composition or marketplace functions.

## 3. Explicit falsifiers

Market Discovery v0 should revise or reject the current direction if evidence shows one or more of the following:

- public sources do not produce sufficiently discriminating problem signals;
- outreach still requires generic volume prospecting to generate conversations;
- target organizations recognize the problem but do not assign budget or urgency to it;
- the proposed work is indistinguishable from commodity consulting;
- delivery cost or required expert effort destroys contribution margin;
- every case is unique enough that reusable capabilities do not emerge;
- paid data/tools improve convenience but not enough decision value to justify their recurring cost;
- client trust, confidentiality, liability or procurement friction blocks the proposed delivery model;
- the useful unit of value turns out to be different from the current “engineering evidence sprint” hypothesis.

A negative result is a valid outcome and must update the model rather than be reframed as a success.

## 4. Initial market scope

Start in France and, when useful, EU public-procurement visibility because the source landscape is rich and low-cost.

### Initial organization families

1. Industrial / digital SMEs with complex delivery obligations.
2. Mid-sized engineering and systems-integration firms.
3. Companies participating in public tenders involving software, cyber, identity, testing, infrastructure or complex integration.
4. Organizations undergoing modernization, supplier change, qualification pressure or AI/ML adoption.
5. Acquisition / transition targets already surfaced by the takeover watch when they also expose capabilities, client access or unmet engineering needs.

This scope is intentionally broad enough to learn but narrow enough to avoid mass B2B prospecting.

## 5. Data-source strategy

### Tier 0 — free / public first
Use public or open sources before paying for enrichment:

- Sirene / INSEE;
- RNE / INPI;
- BODACC;
- Annuaire des Entreprises;
- public procurement data, including DECP / data.gouv, BOAMP, PLACE and TED;
- company websites, public documentation, hiring pages and technical publications;
- public standards/certification information when relevant;
- Actify and legitimate company-transmission sources for acquisition/turnaround cases.

### Tier 1 — low-cost enrichment only after a gap is explicit
A commercial source may be tested only when Market Discovery records a concrete missing field that materially affects target selection, contactability or decision confidence.

The test must record:

- missing information;
- current workaround;
- expected decision/time value;
- subscription or per-use cost;
- measured incremental yield after trial.

### Tier 2 — premium databases
Do not subscribe to premium global corporate data merely to increase lead volume. Promote only if repeated cases establish a measurable economic advantage.

## 6. Signal catalogue v0

Each candidate must be linked to at least one observable signal and one hypothesized problem. Candidate signals include:

- newly published complex tender / award;
- repeated wins/losses in a technical procurement family;
- large or unusual hiring burst in systems, testing, DevSecOps, AI/ML or integration;
- certification / regulatory / qualification obligations;
- announced modernization, migration or transformation;
- acquisition, turnaround or management transition;
- supplier dependency / supply-chain change;
- complex software/hardware/system integration;
- explicit performance, resilience or scale commitment;
- AI/ML deployment with weak visible assurance or governance;
- evidence-heavy customer obligations;
- engineering growth with scarce specialist roles;
- multi-site / multi-supplier delivery complexity;
- public incidents or delivery friction, when factually established and appropriate to use.

Signals are evidence of *possible* need, not proof of budget or pain.

## 7. Candidate record

For every retained organization, capture:

- organization and legal identity;
- source URLs / source dates;
- observed signal(s);
- inferred problem statement;
- confidence and competing explanations;
- relevant capability hypothesis;
- role classification: client / design partner / acquisition target / consolidation partner / expert provider / tech-data provider / research partner;
- plausible buyer / accountable function, when publicly inferable;
- smallest credible first conversation;
- smallest credible first paid intervention;
- expected delivery inputs;
- external expertise likely required;
- confidentiality / regulatory / procurement constraints;
- reasons not to pursue;
- current disposition: observe / research / contact / interview / propose / pilot / reject / revisit.

No candidate should enter active outreach on the basis of a generic industry fit alone.

## 8. Capability hypotheses v0

Do not freeze a product catalogue yet. Start with a small set of problem-oriented capabilities already supported by existing work:

### H-CAP-01 — Evidence-guided complex engineering diagnostic
Clarify a complex technical decision, map claims and assumptions, identify missing evidence, compare options, expose uncertainty and define a reversible next action.

### H-CAP-02 — Requirements / RFP / contract intelligence
Extract, normalize, group, compare and challenge requirements; trace obligations to architecture, delivery, tests and response evidence; assist a bounded response strategy.

### H-CAP-03 — Qualification and test evidence design
Move from requirements / architecture / BOM / supplier evidence to a proportionate qualification strategy, environment profile, evidence plan and residual-risk view.

### H-CAP-04 — AI/ML engineering assurance
Assess whether an AI/ML proposal is genuinely supported by data, baselines, validation, deployment controls and decision-relevant metrics; define the cheapest falsification or pilot that reduces uncertainty.

### H-CAP-05 — Integration / delivery system design
Challenge integration strategies, interfaces, dependencies, resilience, observability, CI/CD, infrastructure and environment-provisioning assumptions.

These are hypotheses. A capability survives only if real conversations and paid work show repeated value.

## 9. Initial offer hypotheses

The first commercial unit should be bounded and outcome-based.

Possible forms to test:

- short paid diagnostic;
- evidence-guided engineering sprint;
- RFP / architecture / qualification challenge;
- decision memo + evidence gap plan;
- bounded design review with optional specialist participation.

Do not publish a large service catalogue in v0. Interview evidence should determine which one or two offers deserve packaging.

## 10. Discovery interview protocol

Each conversation should try to establish, in this order:

1. What consequential decision or delivery problem exists now?
2. What happens if it is not solved?
3. Who owns the outcome and budget?
4. What has already been tried?
5. Which evidence is trusted today, and which is missing?
6. What part is difficult because of complexity, fragmentation, scarce expertise, uncertainty or cost?
7. What would a useful bounded result look like?
8. What would make the organization refuse such an intervention?
9. What procurement / confidentiality / liability constraints matter?
10. Would they allocate time, data, sponsorship and eventually money to a bounded next step?

Do not lead with the future marketplace or agent architecture unless the interlocutor's problem makes that mechanism relevant.

## 11. Funnel experiment v0

Starting hypothesis:

- 20 qualified problem records;
- 10 discovery conversations;
- 3 concrete proposals;
- 1 paid pilot.

Track conversion at every stage, but do not optimize vanity volume.

For each loss, record the dominant reason:

- wrong problem;
- weak urgency;
- no budget;
- wrong buyer;
- insufficient credibility;
- procurement friction;
- confidentiality constraints;
- incumbent provider;
- offer too broad;
- offer too narrow;
- price mismatch;
- timing;
- other evidence-backed reason.

## 12. Economic model v0

For every proposed or executed intervention, record:

`Revenue`

minus

- external human expert cost;
- data/API cost;
- compute / AI subscription or usage attributable to the intervention;
- travel and other direct costs;
- subcontracting;
- other variable delivery cost.

This yields contribution margin before fixed overhead.

Also measure:

- founder/lead engineering hours;
- specialist hours;
- AI-assisted hours saved or added;
- cycle time;
- rework;
- client-perceived usefulness;
- whether the output changed a decision or avoided work/risk;
- probability of repeat or referral.

Do not hide founder time merely because it has no immediate cash outflow.

## 13. Buy-vs-build evidence rule for tools and data

A paid tool becomes a candidate fixed cost only when at least one of these is demonstrated:

- it unlocks otherwise unreachable opportunities;
- it materially improves conversion quality;
- it reduces paid human effort enough to exceed its cost;
- it provides required evidence unavailable elsewhere;
- it supports repeated delivery across enough paid work to produce positive expected value.

The first economic target is therefore not “upgrade the stack”; it is to make the activity capable of funding its own next justified tool.

## 14. Acquisition / transition integration

The existing acquisition watch remains a separate decision track but shares the same evidence graph.

For each takeover/turnaround candidate, Market Discovery may additionally ask:

- Does this firm expose unmet client problems that validate a capability hypothesis?
- Does it own a scarce capability, certification, customer access or delivery asset that would be expensive to build organically?
- Could it become a future expert/capability provider even if acquisition is rejected?
- Does distress or transition create misleading signals that should not be interpreted as market demand?

Acquisition attractiveness and customer-market attractiveness must remain separately scored.

## 15. Human+AI capability ecosystem learning

When a real opportunity requires expertise beyond the lead practitioner's scope, capture a capability gap record:

`problem → missing capability → evidence/authority required → potential expert(s) → permitted AI assistance → tools/connectors → deliverable → accountable human authority`.

This is the raw material for any future marketplace. No marketplace feature should be built unless repeated gap records justify it.

## 16. Governance and ethics

- Use lawful, proportionate data collection.
- Separate public fact from inference.
- Do not infer sensitive personal characteristics.
- Avoid deceptive personalization or mass unsolicited automation.
- Preserve confidentiality boundaries.
- Do not represent AI assistance as professional authority it does not possess.
- Record conflicts of interest when an organization is simultaneously a potential client, acquisition target or provider.
- Keep human accountability explicit for consequential decisions.

## 18. First execution sequence

### Phase 0 — baseline and governance
- preserve prior context;
- define candidate and evidence records;
- define source and privacy rules;
- establish explicit falsifiers.

### Phase 1 — 20 evidence-backed candidates
- collect 20 problem records from public/open sources;
- intentionally include different organization types;
- rate problem evidence and capability fit;
- reject weak generic leads.

### Phase 2 — qualitative discovery
- conduct up to 10 conversations;
- update problem vocabulary, buyer map, urgency and procurement constraints;
- revise capability hypotheses.

### Phase 3 — paid-offer test
- produce up to 3 bounded proposals;
- require explicit deliverable, assumptions, data needs, price, effort and stop criteria;
- seek at least one paid pilot.

### Phase 4 — replay and economic decision
- compare forecast vs actual effort/value;
- calculate contribution margin;
- record reusable components and capability gaps;
- decide what to stop, revise, package or automate next.

## 19. Initial decision gates

### G0 — Baseline recorded
Pass when prior assumptions and existing acquisition logic are traced without silently rewriting them.

### G1 — Candidate protocol usable
Pass when another reviewer could understand why a candidate was retained or rejected from sources and recorded inference.

### G2 — Signal quality demonstrated
Pass when the first candidate set contains observable problem evidence rather than generic firmographics only.

### G3 — Interview evidence obtained
Pass when conversations materially update at least one prior belief, offer or buyer assumption.

### G4 — Willingness-to-pay tested
Pass only through a real commercial test, not verbal enthusiasm alone.

### G5 — Delivery economics observed
Pass when actual direct costs, lead time and human effort are recorded for a pilot.

### G6 — Reuse decision justified
Pass when at least one repeatable capability, tool, data source or expert role is supported by multiple cases — or when the evidence says none should yet be productized.

## 20. Immediate next actions

1. Add the candidate record and scoring rubric.
2. Build the first 20-candidate sample from open/public sources, not paid databases.
3. Include acquisition/transition targets where relevant but score acquisition and client/design-partner potential separately.
4. Use the first sample to decide which two capability hypotheses deserve interview testing first.
5. Only then prepare outreach and interview execution.

## 21. Success criterion for v0

The strongest v0 outcome is not necessarily a successful sale. It is a **defensible update of the business prior** supported by real market evidence.

A paid pilot with positive economics would be strong evidence to continue. A clean falsification of the current offer, buyer or market hypothesis is also valuable if it prevents premature spending on subscriptions, marketplace infrastructure or product development.
